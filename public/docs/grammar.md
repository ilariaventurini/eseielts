# connecting ideas: linkers part 1 (2026-06-29)

memory trick for you
furthermore = what's more (stronger addition)
in addition = also (adds another point)
however = but (connects sentences or clauses)
despite = in spite of (followed by a noun or -ing)
therefore = for that reason (logical conclusion)
as a result = because of that, this happened (focus on the effect)
since = because (followed by a complete clause)
due to = because of (followed by a noun or noun phrase)

## furthermore (adding information)
**position**: at the beginning of a sentence, followed by a comma
**use when**: adding a stronger or more important point
**formality**: more formal, good for writing and presentations
**what they mean**: these linkers help you add extra information or another point to what you've already said.

examples:
- *the apartment is close to my work. furthermore, it has a beautiful view*
- *the course is very practical. furthermore, it's taught by industry experts*
- *one advantage of my job is the flexible hours. furthermore, the office is close to my home*

## in addition  (adding information)
**position**: at the beginning of a sentence, followed by a comma
**use when**: adding extra information of similar importance
**formality**: neutral, suitable for both speaking and writing
**what they mean**: these linkers help you add extra information or another point to what you've already said

examples:
- *the hotel has a swimming pool. in addition, there's a gym for guests*
- *she speaks french fluently. in addition, she knows some spanish*
- *i enjoy living in this neighborhood because it's quiet. in addition, there are many parks nearby*

## however (showing contrast)
**position**: at the beginning of a sentence (followed by a comma) or between two sentences (with a period before it)
**use when**: showing a strong contrast with what was said before
**formality**: neutral to formal
**what they mean**: these linkers show contrast or unexpected information – when something is different from what you might expect.

examples:
- *i like the apartment. however, it's too expensive*
- *she studied hard for the exam. however, she didn't pass*
- *i liked my previous job. however, the hours were too long*

## despite (showing contrast)
structure: despite + noun/gerund (not a complete clause with subject + verb)
**use when**: showing something happened even though there was a good reason it shouldn't
**formality**: neutral to formal (more formal than `however`)
**what they mean**: these linkers show contrast or unexpected information – when something is different from what you might expect.

examples:
- *despite the rain, we enjoyed our walk* (not "despite it was raining")
- *despite having a broken leg, she came to work*
- *despite the difficulty of the course, i managed to pass the exam*

## therefore (showing consequences)
**position**: between two sentences (with a period or semicolon before it)
**use when**: showing a logical conclusion or result
**formality**: more formal, good for academic or professional contexts
**what they mean**: these linkers show that one thing happened because of another – they connect a cause with its effect.

examples:
- *the bus was late. therefore, i missed the meeting*
- *the evidence was clear; therefore, they made their decision*
- *i missed the train; therefore, i was late for the interview*

## as a result (showing consequences)
**position**: at the beginning of a sentence, followed by a comma
**use when**: showing a direct consequence of what happened before
**formality**: neutral, works in both conversation and writing
**what they mean**: these linkers show that one thing happened because of another – they connect a cause with its effect.

examples:
- *it rained all weekend. as a result, our camping trip was canceled*
- *he didn't study for the test. as a result, he failed*
- *i practiced speaking english every day. as a result, my confidence improved*

## since (explaining reasons)
**position**: can be at the beginning or in the middle of a sentence
**structure**: since + complete clause (subject + verb)
**use when**: giving a reason that might already be known or obvious
**formality**: more formal than `because`
**what they mean**: these linkers help you explain why something happens - they give reasons for actions, decisions, or situations.

examples:
- *since the weather was nice, we had lunch in the garden*
- *i took a taxi since i was running late*
- *i decided to change my job since i wanted a new challenge*

## due to (explaining reasons)
**structure**: due to + noun phrase (not a complete clause)
**use when**: explaining that something happened because of a particular factor
**formality**: neutral to formal, good for writing and presentations
**what they mean**: these linkers help you explain why something happens - they give reasons for actions, decisions, or situations.

examples:
- *the delay was due to bad weather* (not "due to it was raining")
- *due to the traffic, i missed the beginning of the movie*
- *i moved to a new apartment due to the noise in my old neighborhood*

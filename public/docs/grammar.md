# connecting ideas: linkers part 1 (2026-06-29)

memory trick for you
furthermore = what's more (stronger addition)
in addition = also (adds another point)
however = but (connects sentences or clauses)
despite = in spite of (followed by a noun or -ing)
therefore = for that reason (logical conclusion)
as a result = because of that, this happened (focus on the effect)
since = because (followed by a complete clause)
due to = because of (followed by a noun or noun phrase)

## furthermore (adding information) = inoltre
**position**: at the beginning of a sentence, followed by a comma
**use when**: adding a stronger or more important point
**formality**: more formal, good for writing and presentations
**what they mean**: these linkers help you add extra information or another point to what you've already said.

examples:
- *the apartment is close to my work. furthermore, it has a beautiful view*
- *the course is very practical. furthermore, it's taught by industry experts*
- *one advantage of my job is the flexible hours. furthermore, the office is close to my home*

## in addition  (adding information) = in aggiunta
**position**: at the beginning of a sentence, followed by a comma
**use when**: adding extra information of similar importance
**formality**: neutral, suitable for both speaking and writing
**what they mean**: these linkers help you add extra information or another point to what you've already said

examples:
- *the hotel has a swimming pool. in addition, there's a gym for guests*
- *she speaks french fluently. in addition, she knows some spanish*
- *i enjoy living in this neighborhood because it's quiet. in addition, there are many parks nearby*

## however (showing contrast) = tuttavia
**position**: at the beginning of a sentence (followed by a comma) or between two sentences (with a period before it)
**use when**: showing a strong contrast with what was said before
**formality**: neutral to formal
**what they mean**: these linkers show contrast or unexpected information – when something is different from what you might expect.

examples:
- *i like the apartment. however, it's too expensive*
- *she studied hard for the exam. however, she didn't pass*
- *i liked my previous job. however, the hours were too long*

## despite (showing contrast) = nonostante
structure: despite + noun/gerund (not a complete clause with subject + verb)
**use when**: showing something happened even though there was a good reason it shouldn't
**formality**: neutral to formal (more formal than `however`)
**what they mean**: these linkers show contrast or unexpected information – when something is different from what you might expect.

examples:
- *despite the rain, we enjoyed our walk* (not "despite it was raining")
- *despite having a broken leg, she came to work*
- *despite the difficulty of the course, i managed to pass the exam*

## therefore (showing consequences) = quindi
**position**: between two sentences (with a period or semicolon before it)
**use when**: showing a logical conclusion or result
**formality**: more formal, good for academic or professional contexts
**what they mean**: these linkers show that one thing happened because of another – they connect a cause with its effec
examples:
- *the bus was late. therefore, i missed the meeting*
- *the evidence was clear; therefore, they made their decision*
- *i missed the train; therefore, i was late for the interview*

## as a result (showing consequences) = di conseguenza
**position**: at the beginning of a sentence, followed by a comma
**use when**: showing a direct consequence of what happened before
**formality**: neutral, works in both conversation and writing
**what they mean**: these linkers show that one thing happened because of another – they connect a cause with its effect.

examples:
- *it rained all weekend. as a result, our camping trip was canceled*
- *he didn't study for the test. as a result, he failed*
- *i practiced speaking english every day. as a result, my confidence improved*

## since (explaining reasons) = dato che/poichè
**position**: can be at the beginning or in the middle of a sentence
**structure**: since + complete clause (subject + verb)
**use when**: giving a reason that might already be known or obvious
**formality**: more formal than `because`
**what they mean**: these linkers help you explain why something happens - they give reasons for actions, decisions, or situations.

examples:
- *since the weather was nice, we had lunch in the garden*
- *i took a taxi since i was running late*
- *i decided to change my job since i wanted a new challenge*

## due to (explaining reasons) = a causa di
**structure**: due to + noun phrase (not a complete clause)
**use when**: explaining that something happened because of a particular factor
**formality**: neutral to formal, good for writing and presentations
**what they mean**: these linkers help you explain why something happens - they give reasons for actions, decisions, or situations.

examples:
- *the delay was due to bad weather* (not "due to it was raining")
- *due to the traffic, i missed the beginning of the movie*
- *i moved to a new apartment due to the noise in my old neighborhood*

# connecting ideas: linkers part 2 (2026-07-07)

## to/in order to (purpose) = traduzione
**structure**: to + verb/in order to + verb
**use when**: explaining the purpose of an action
**difference**: "in order to" is slightly more formal
**what they mean**: these linkers help you explain the purpose or goal behind your actions.

examples:
- *i went to the shop to buy some milk*
- *she studied all night in order to pass the exam*
- *i practice english every day to improve my fluency*
- *I am studying every day to learn English*
- *She woke up early in order to catch the train*

## so that (purpose) = traduzione
**structure**: so that + subject + verb (often with can/could)
**use when**: explaining purpose with more detail
**note**: needs a complete clause after it
**what they mean**: these linkers help you explain the purpose or goal behind your actions.

examples:
- *i'll speak slowly so that you can understand me*
- *we left early so that we wouldn't miss the train*
- *i always write shopping lists so that i don't forget anything*
- *the teacher give us simple examples so that we can understand easily*
- *Put on your coat so that you won't feel cold*

## while/when (time relationship) = traduzione
**structure**: while/when + subject + verb
**use when**: showing actions that happen at the same time (while) or at a point in time (when)
**difference**: "while" is for ongoing actions; "when" is for points or periods
**what they mean**: these linkers show how events relate to each other in time - before, after, or during..

examples:
- *i listen to music while i cook*
- *when i arrived home, i found a package at the door*
- *when i get home, i first change into comfortable clothes*
- *I like to listen to music while I cook dinner*
- *My phone rang while I was watching a movie*

## before/after (time relationship) = traduzione
**structure**: before/after + noun or verb-ing
**use when**: showing one event happened earlier or later than another
**what they mean**: these linkers show how events relate to each other in time - before, after, or during

examples:
- *i always have coffee before leaving for work*
- *let's talk after the meeting*
- *before going to work, i always check my emails*
- *Wash your hands before you eat*
- *I always check my bag before I leave the house*
- *We can go for a walk after it stops raining.*
- *After breakfast, I brush my teeth.*

## for example/for instance (clarification) = traduzione
**position**: usually after a comma, semicolon, or period
**use when**: giving specific examples to support your point
**what they mean**: these linkers help you explain, give examples, or clarify your point

examples:
- *i enjoy outdoor activities; for example, hiking and cycling*
- *some cities are very expensive. for instance, tokyo and new york*
- *i prefer asian cuisine; for example, thai and japanese food*
- *You can do many things in London. For instance, you can visit free museums*
- *I love healthy food. For example, I eat a lot of fruit and vegetables*

## actually/on the contrary (clarification) = traduzione
**position**: usually at the beginning of a sentence or after a comma
**use when**: contradicting or correcting information
**difference**: "actually" clarifies information; "on the contrary" shows complete opposition
**what they mean**: these linkers help you explain, give examples, or clarify your point

examples:
- *i'm not french. actually, i'm swiss*
- *it's not expensive. on the contrary, it's quite cheap*
- *people think i'm shy. actually, i'm just a good listener*
- *I'm sorry, I actually forgot to buy the milk!*
- *People think I am British, but actually, I am Canadian*
- *The exam was not difficult. On the contrary, it was very easy!*

## by the way/talking of (conversation management linkers) = traduzione
**position**: usually at the beginning of a sentence
**use when**: changing the subject or making a related point
**what they mean**: these expressions help you navigate conversations smoothly - changing topics, returning to points, or ending discussions.

examples:
- *i've got my driving test next week. by the way, did you pass your driving test?*
- *the music at the party was great. talking of music, have you heard the new album?*
- *i like your new phone. by the way, can you recommend a good case for it?*
- *this restaurant is excellent. talking of restaurants, have you tried the new italian place?*
- *I loved the movie last night. By the way, did you finish your homework?*
- *A: "I saw a beautiful dog today." B: "Talking of dogs, how is your puppy?"*

## anyway (conversation management linkers) = traduzione
**position**: usually at the beginning of a sentence
**use when**:
- returning to the main topic
- supporting previous points
- ending a conversation - *anyway, i must go. it's very late*
**what they mean**: these expressions help you navigate conversations smoothly - changing topics, returning to points, or ending discussions.

examples:
- *anyway, what were you saying about harry?*
- *i'm too tired to go out. anyway, there's a good film on tv.*
- *The weather was bad, but anyway, we had a great time at the party.*

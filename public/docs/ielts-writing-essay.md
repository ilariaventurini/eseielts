# writing - essay (task 2)

## essay types

Five types — you will not know which you get; prepare for all. Recycled topics may be **reworded** or become a **different essay type** (e.g. discussion → opinion). Instructions change the essay even when the topic stays the same:

1. **Opinion Essay** — agree / disagree / partial agreement
2. **Discussion Essay** — discuss both sides
3. **Advantage / Disadvantage** — includes outweigh essay
4. **Cause / Problem / Solution Essays**
5. **Direct Question Essays** — positive/negative development / one, two, or three questions

**Example — same topic, different instructions:**

1. Some people think that art is no longer important and funding should be diverted to the development of science and technology. Do you agree or disagree?
2. Some people think the funding for art should be diverted to science and technology, but others disagree. Discuss both sides and give your opinion.
3. Some people think that art is no longer important and funding should be diverted to the development of science and technology. Do you think art is important? Should art still be funded as much as it is?

## advertising to children in schools
*Q: fast food companies give money to schools to advertise in them — positive or negative?*

**for:** company donations fund extra resources/facilities; children learn money management and to be selective buyers
**against:** promotes junk food → poor diet, long-term health costs; children are easily influenced, cannot distinguish ads from education, cannot form their own opinions; ads target parents via children; expensive products create unrealistic demands

## employment competition (young vs old)
*Q: younger people compete with older people for the same jobs — problems + solutions (or causes + solutions)*

**problems:** older workers who cannot compete may lose jobs → poverty; growing elderly population = financial crisis; longer working lives leave fewer jobs for youth
**solutions:** adequate retirement benefits; fixed retirement age; job quotas by age; job-sharing between generations; employ older workers as mentors/knowledge-passers rather than position-takers

## the importance of history
*Q: studying history is a waste of time vs essential — discuss both*

**against history:** memorising dates/facts is useless; same mistakes repeat → history is irrelevant; better to study science/technology; events have multiple interpretations; curricula include little recent history
**for history:** builds cultural identity and belonging; teaches what ancestors suffered/achieved; prevents repeating tragedies (e.g. Holocaust); helps understand change, patterns, and future trends; preserves useful knowledge (e.g. traditional medicine)

## housing & trees in urban areas
*Q: planting trees in open city spaces is more important than building houses — agree/disagree?*

**for trees:** peaceful environment, better mental health; oxygen production, cleaner air; shade reduces heat; brings nature to cities; noise buffer; may reduce crime by encouraging community activity; supports urban wildlife
**for housing:** lack of affordable housing → rising crime; cities need housing to grow/develop; workers need affordable homes; shelter is a basic need; urban space is limited — priority to shelter over parks; without enough homes → homelessness, slums

## international aid & celebrities
*Q: famous people’s support for aid organisations draws attention vs makes problems less important — discuss both*

**for celebrity involvement:** raises profile of charities/causes (e.g. Princess Diana and landmines); familiar voices are more trustworthy and compelling than unknown advocates
**against:** media focus shifts to the celebrity, not the cause; message feels shallow; promotes world inequality; charity becomes dependent on celebrity’s reputation; some people reject the cause if they dislike the celebrity; interest drops when celebrity withdraws

## salaries (high earners)
*Q: some earn extremely high salaries — good for a country vs government should cap salaries — discuss both*

**high salaries are good:** reward skill/talent; incentive to work harder; attract people to dangerous/unpleasant jobs; sign of national wealth; express gratitude for expertise; deter brain drain
**cap salaries:** distribute wealth evenly; more equality; avoid demoralising low earners; prevent rich from unfair societal control; free funds for public services

## function of schools
*Q: schools only turn children into good citizens/workers vs benefit them as individuals — agree/disagree?*

**schools as socialisation:** teach discipline for the workplace; right vs wrong; shared cultural values; job-market skills
**schools as individual development:** help discover potential; choose suitable subjects; trained teachers develop strengths/weaknesses; build confidence via lessons and extracurriculars; understand the world

## female staff in senior positions
*Q: companies should allocate a percentage of senior roles to women — agree/disagree?*

**for quotas:** equal rights/opportunities; avoids discrimination; better gender balance; supportive for other female employees; women bring communication/diplomacy skills
**against quotas:** unrealistic to stipulate percentages; may lack suitable female applicants; forces hiring to detriment; senior roles should be based on experience/skills/qualifications, not gender

## littering in cities
*Q: steeper fines is the best way to stop littering — agree/disagree?*

**analysis:** litter = dropping waste instead of using bins; people litter for convenience and because they are rarely caught
**solutions:** bigger fines (deter if enforced); **education** of young generation (long-term — awareness + habit); **more bins** (convenience + reminder); more police as deterrent

## banning mobile phones in public places
*Q: mobiles should be banned in libraries, shops, public transport — agree/disagree?*

**for ban:** intrusive/uninvited noise; disturb people concentrating; antisocial (texting instead of talking); interfere with hospital equipment; ruin experience in libraries/galleries; recording invades privacy
**against ban:** silent/vibrate mode is non-intrusive; useful in emergencies; entertainment on long journeys; multi-functional (banking, research, texting); safety net for children contacting parents
**concession:** ban only where they break rules or interfere (e.g. libraries); allow elsewhere with respect for others

## tourism and local communities
*Q: tourism is beneficial and should be encouraged — advantages/disadvantages*

**advantages:** boosts revenue and economy; creates jobs; stimulates entrepreneurship; fresh cultural perspectives; cross-cultural understanding; locals learn English; infrastructure improvements benefit whole community; preserves/restores historic and natural sites
**disadvantages:** economic dependency if unsustainable; seasonal employment; pollution (noise, air); friction if local culture disrespected; cultural erosion; profits go to tour operators, not locals; rising housing/living costs

## traffic & pollution
*Q: raising petrol price is the best way to solve traffic and pollution — agree/disagree + other measures*

**petrol price increase:** fewer people afford petrol → fewer cars → less congestion and air pollution
**other measures:** improve public transport (more routes, punctuality, cheaper tickets); car-free/no-traffic zones in city centres

## handwriting skills for children
*Q: teaching handwriting is important — agree/disagree?*

**for handwriting:** basic tool for tests, notes, homework; poor handwriting hurts school performance; linked to spelling, reading, grammar; early habits essential; sometimes better than typing; cognition link — writing by hand aids learning
**against / alternatives:** teach touch-typing instead (more useful today); time better spent on other skills; spell-check reduces need to focus on handwriting

## older or younger leaders
*Q: younger people should be leaders rather than older directors/managers — agree/disagree?*

**young leaders:** up-to-date with modern world; more dynamic, forward-thinking, open to new ideas; stronger and healthier for leadership demands
**older leaders:** experience is critical; more authority to manage others; sense of responsibility often lacking in the young

## public services vs the arts
*Q: government should spend more on public services than on the arts — agree/disagree?*

**public services** (schools, healthcare, transport): need funding — explain why (health access, reliable transport, etc.)
**the arts** (museums, galleries, theatre): express culture; often underfunded — cutting arts funding may kill them entirely
**structures:** one-sided (fund services, arts still important) or balanced (fund both — services need more but arts still matter)
